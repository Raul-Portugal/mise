Here is a comprehensive product, architectural, and UI/UX design plan for "Mise" (short for mise en place)—a minimal, tactile, browser-based recipe and cooking companion.1. Visual Identity & UI/UX PhilosophyThe design ethos is "Warm Editorial Minimalism"—inspired by print cookbooks (like Kinfolk or Cereal magazine), Braun hardware aesthetics, and high-craft digital apps (like Things 3 or iA Writer).Color Palette ("Warm Kitchen"):Background (Primary): Alabaster / Warm Paper (#FAF8F5) — reduces eye strain compared to sterile #FFFFFF.Surface / Cards: Stone White (#F3EFEA) with subtle 1px borders (#E6E1DA).Typography (Primary): Espresso Charcoal (#1C1B18).Typography (Muted): Warm Grey (#78736A).Primary Accent: Terracotta / Burnt Sienna (#C85A32) — used sparingly for active states, timers, and primary CTAs.Secondary Accent: Sage Herb (#4A6B53) — used for completion checks and metric/imperial badges.Cooking Mode (Dark Theme): Deep Cast Iron (#141413) background with High-Contrast Cream (#F5F2EB) text for readability from across a kitchen counter.Typography:Recipe Titles & Editorial Headers: A high-contrast serif (e.g., Newsreader, Instrument Serif, or Playfair Display).UI & Instructions: Clean humanist sans-serif (e.g., Inter or Plus Jakarta Sans).Quantities, Units & Timers: Tabular monospace (e.g., JetBrains Mono or font-variant-numeric: tabular-nums) so numbers align cleanly in ingredient columns and don't jitter during live conversion or countdowns.Mobile-First Ergonomics:Bottom-anchored navigation bar and action sheets (within easy thumb reach).Generous touch targets (minimum 48x48px) for messy or wet hands.2. Core Feature 1: High-Accuracy Culinary Conversion EngineStandard apps fail at conversions because they convert volume to volume (1 cup flour = 236.59 ml), whereas bakers and cooks convert Imperial Volume to Metric Weight (1 cup all-purpose flour = 120g) and need culinary rounding, not raw floating-point math.A. The Data Source Strategy (USDA + King Arthur Baking)To achieve maximum accuracy, the app uses a two-tier density resolution engine:Tier 1: Curated Culinary Density Matrix (Bundled Local JSON - Instant/Offline):Sourced from the King Arthur Baking Ingredient Weight Chart and USDA FoodData Central (SR Legacy) standard weights.Contains ~500 core kitchen ingredients mapped to grams per 1 US Standard Cup (236.588 ml) along with state modifiers (e.g., sifted, packed, chopped, melted).Examples:All-Purpose Flour: 120g / cup (0.507 g/ml)Granulated Sugar: 198g / cup (0.837 g/ml)Brown Sugar (Packed): 213g / cup (0.900 g/ml)Kosher Salt (Diamond Crystal) vs. Table Salt: 1 tbsp = 9g vs 18g (crucial distinction!).Tier 2: Live USDA FoodData Central API Fallback:If a user enters an uncommon ingredient not in the local cache, the app queries the free USDA FoodData Central API (/v1/foods/search), inspects the foodPortions array (which maps household measures like "1 cup" or "1 tbsp" to exact gram weights), and caches that density locally in IndexedDB.B. Smart Conversion & "Appropriate Approximation" EngineRaw math produces unusable recipes (e.g., 1 cup milk = 236.588 ml or 1 oz butter = 28.3495g). Our engine applies Culinary Rounding Rules:Conversion Modes:Original (As written)Metric Weight-First (Converts dry/solid volumes like cups of flour/butter/sugar into grams, and liquids like water/milk/stock into ml or grams).Imperial / US Customary (Converts grams/ml into oz, lb, cups, tbsp, tsp).Precision & Rounding Tiers:Baking / High-Precision Ingredients (yeast, baking powder, salt, spices, agar, < 10g): Round to nearest 0.1g or 1/8 tsp.Standard Solids (10g – 100g): Round to nearest 1g.Bulk Solids (> 100g) & Liquids (> 100ml): Round to nearest 5g / 5ml (e.g., 118.29g becomes 120g, matching standard recipe scales; an optional "Exact" toggle reveals raw values on tap).Imperial Fraction Snapper: Converts decimal ounces/cups into human-readable kitchen fractions (1/4, 1/3, 1/2, 2/3, 3/4) plus remainders (e.g., instead of 0.625 cups, displays 1/2 cup + 2 tbsp).Temperature & Pan Size Conversions:Inline detection in recipe steps via regex/NLP: automatically converts 350°F $\leftrightarrow$ 175°C (or 180°C standard oven notch) and 9x13-inch pan $\leftrightarrow$ 23x33 cm pan.3. Core Feature 2: Recipe Creation & Multi-Format ImportUsers shouldn't have to fill out 20 tedious form fields to add a recipe.A. Smart Text-First Editor ("Paste & Parse")Natural Language Input: Users can type or paste raw text lines like:1 1/2 cups (180g) all-purpose flour, siftedReal-Time Tokenizer: As they type, an NLP parser (like parse-ingredient combined with custom regex) highlights the Quantity (1.5), Unit (cup), Ingredient Entity (all-purpose flour — matched to the density database with a subtle green dot), and Prep Note (sifted).B. Frictionless Import / Export FormatsURL / Web Recipe Importer (schema.org/Recipe LD+JSON):Almost every major food blog (NYT Cooking, Serious Eats, Bon Appétit, BBC Good Food) embeds a hidden <script type="application/ld+json"> tag containing structured schema.org/Recipe data.Users can paste a URL (or raw HTML/JSON), and the app strips away all life stories and ads, extracting title, yield, prep/cook time, structured ingredients, and steps.Native .json & .cook (Cooklang) Support:JSON: Full support for importing/exporting single recipes or whole recipe books in standard Schema.org Recipe JSON format.Markdown / Cooklang (.md / .cook): Power users love plain text. Supporting Cooklang syntax (Add @flour{120%g} and mix for ~{5%minutes}) makes writing and sharing recipes lightning-fast.4. Core Feature 3: "Cooking Mode" (Hands-Free Kitchen UX)When the user taps "Start Cooking", the UI transforms from an editorial reader into a high-utility kitchen instrument:Screen Wake Lock API (navigator.wakeLock):Prevents the phone or tablet screen from dimming or locking while the user's hands are covered in dough or oil.Split / Focused Step-by-Step Layout:Large Typography: Current step is displayed at 24px–32px font size.Inline Ingredient Injection: Never scroll back up to check how much sugar goes in Step 4! Cooking Mode automatically injects the converted quantity directly into the step text (e.g., "Whisk together the flour (120g) and sugar (50g) in a large bowl").Interactive Mise-en-Place Checklist:Before Step 1, a "Step 0: Mise en Place" screen lets the user check off ingredients and required cookware as they prep.One-Tap Contextual Timers:Any time duration mentioned in a step (e.g., "simmer for 12–15 minutes") is rendered as a clickable pill button (⏱ 12:00).Tapping starts a persistent floating timer dock at the bottom of the screen (supporting multiple concurrent timers labeled by step, e.g., "Pasta - 08:14", "Garlic Bread - 03:20") with Web Audio API chime alerts.Gesture & Keyboard Navigation:Full-width left/right tap zones or swipe gestures on mobile; Spacebar / Arrow Keys on desktop/tablet.5.
